Things fall apart at the rate we stop noticing

Most failure isn’t breakage. It’s neglect. Nobody broke the thing. Everyone assumed someone else was watching it.

Maintenance starts with noticing. You can’t repair what you haven’t seen.

Examples:

  • A community fridge stays healthy because someone sees the spoiled produce and throws it out.
  • An open-source library stops getting patches. Nobody decided to abandon it.
  • A shared inbox fills up because every reader assumes another reader replied.

Open loops are the personal version. Open them faster than you close them and everything goes stale. Closing a loop is maintenance.

Design for noticing:

  • Make decay visible. Stale things should look stale.
  • Make the owner visible. Someone should be named.
  • Make flagging cheap. “This feels off” should take one step.
  • Put checking on a rhythm. Noticing shouldn’t depend on luck.

Most systems budget for building. Budget for noticing too.
