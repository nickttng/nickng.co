Wear is a record of use that nobody wrote down. It builds up where people actually go, again and again, over years.

Examples:

- A stain above a hat peg, from a hat hung up wet for decades.
- A stair that creaks on the one spot everyone steps.
- A dirt path across a lawn where the sidewalk took the long way.
- A sticky note on a monitor, listing the steps the software should have explained.

Wear has properties other research doesn’t:

- Long horizon. It captures years, not a session.
- Unobserved. It records what people do when nobody is watching.
- Hard to fake. It takes repetition to produce.
- Free. It’s a byproduct of use.

Interviews tell you what people say. Wear tells you what they did.

Software doesn’t wear. A screen looks the same on the thousandth visit as on the first. The traces exist, but you have to choose to leave them visible. Show them to the people who use the thing, not only to the people who own it.

Pave the desire paths.
